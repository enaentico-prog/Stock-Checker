Under the hood

How it learned to read a shelf.

01

Fed it real sales

Two full years of actual store sales — millions of purchases across thousands of products.

02

Boiled it down

Turned each product into a few plain signals: how recently it sold, how steadily, and how many people bought it.

03

Showed it known endings

Trained it on thousands of products where the outcome was already settled — which kept selling and which faded out.

04

Blocked it from cheating

It only ever sees a product's first year when calling its second. It can't peek ahead, so it has to learn real patterns.

05

Tested it on strangers

Checked it against products it had never seen, to make sure it picked up genuine signals and not lucky flukes.

What it found: a product's future comes down mostly to how recently and how widely it sold — not its category or its brand. On top of that, it learned which products get bought together (from 230,000 real baskets) and which ones actually sell faster when discounted. Those two are what decide whether a slow seller is worth saving, and how.

Quick answers

Before you write

  • Does anything I type get uploaded?No. The checker runs entirely in your browser, and the numbers you enter never leave the page. Saved results stay on your device, and messages only go out when you hit send in your own email app.
  • Why did it tell me not to discount?Because that category doesn't sell fast enough on a price cut to cover the margin you'd give up, at your price and cost. Change either one and it recalculates.
  • My category isn't in the list.Pick the closest match. The reading itself doesn't use category at all — only the bundle and discount advice does. Then tell us what's missing.
  • Can I get my data back out?Yes. Export CSV above gives you everything you saved, in a file that opens in Excel.

How your data and messages are handled

  • Nothing uploads on its own. These are plain static pages with no server behind them. Saved results live only in your browser, and feedback goes out by opening your email app — you see the message and press send yourself.
  • Sending feedback shows your email address to whoever receives it, same as any email. Leave the field blank if you don't want a reply; the message still goes through.
  • Clearing your browser data wipes your saved results. They're in local storage, not in an account. Export the CSV if you want to keep them.
  • What you send isn't used automatically. Nothing you log retrains the model, here or anywhere else. Entries would be reviewed by hand before any future update.
  • The training data isn't Philippine. The model learned from a large foreign grocery chain's sales. The approach carries over to a small store; the specific category names, pairings and discount responses may not.
  • The match rate isn't an accuracy score. The figure in your results panel compares the model's call against what you reported on a handful of products. It's a rough personal gut-check, not a real evaluation.
