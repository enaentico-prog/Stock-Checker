Ang daloy · the decision path

Three questions, in the order that matters.

Most tools hand you a chart and leave the thinking to you. This one narrows to a single move, and it only escalates a product that is genuinely fading.

A slow mover is worth saving only if it has a lever you can actually pull. The checker knows two: whether the category genuinely responds to a price cut, and whether shoppers already buy it alongside something else.

How it read the shelf

    Check your slow-movers in one go

    Type them in or upload a filled list. Tag each with a category and enter the three numbers — it reads whether each is worth keeping. The category is for your reference; the reading runs on the sales numbers.

    Download the template, fill it in (it opens in Excel), save it as CSV, then drop it back here. It just reads the numbers you type — it doesn't connect to your till.

    Ang hindi ginagawa ng iba

    It tells you when not to discount.

    Every owner's reflex is to slash the price on a slow item. But plenty of products barely sell more when discounted — and on a thin sari-sari margin, a price cut can lose you money for nothing. Shelf Temp Check runs the math on your cost and price first, then stops you.

    Rescue play · bacon
    Bundle it — don't discount
    ⚠ Huwag mong i-discount 'to. Bacon does respond to price cuts, but at your ₱3 margin a 15% cut would sell you below cost. Pair it instead.

    Panghuling salita · closing words

    You were always going to decide. This just makes it out loud.

    Every tindera already runs this calculation in her head — which one is worth another order, which one has quietly become a loss. The checker does not replace that judgement. It puts numbers behind it, so the call is deliberate instead of postponed, and so the money stuck on your shelf goes back to work. Isang produkto, isang desisyon — then on to the next one.

    Mga paalala · what this tool is and isn't

    • It is a guide, not a guarantee. Every reading is a probability drawn from patterns in past sales, not a prediction about your specific shop. A product read as NATIGIL can still sell; one read as MABENTA can still stall.
    • The training data is not Philippine. The model learned from two years of a large foreign grocery retailer's transactions. The method — recency, steadiness, breadth of buyers — transfers well to a tindahan. The specific category names, pairings and discount responses may not.
    • Peso amounts are yours, not ours. Margin, frozen cash and the discount check are computed purely from the price and cost you type in. Any ₱ figure shown in examples is illustrative.
    • Not financial advice. Decisions about what to stock, discount or drop are yours. Consider spoilage dates, supplier terms and your own customers — things this tool cannot see.
    • Nothing you enter is transmitted. The checker runs entirely in your browser. There is no account, no tracking of your inputs, and no server receiving them.
