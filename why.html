Page 2 of 3 · Why It Matters

You already know which one it is.

The can at the back. The pack of sachets nobody touches. It sold twice in March and it's been sitting there ever since. Most owners hang on to it because tossing it feels like admitting the money's gone. It already is — and it's costing you in three ways at the same time.

Your cash is stuck

Money you can't use

Every peso sitting in stock that won't move is a peso you can't spend on stock that will. The checker shows you exactly how much is tied up in each slow seller.

It's taking up a good spot

Shelf space isn't free

Every slot a dud occupies is a slot a winner can't have. In a small store, space is the tightest thing you've got, and slow sellers hog it.

It's losing value by the week

The clock is running

Food, sachets and seasonal items are worth less every week they sit. Wait long enough and they're worth nothing — then they're trash you paid for.

You're not the only one

It isn't just your shelf. It's the whole economy.

Slow stock feels like a personal screw-up when you're the one staring at it. It isn't. Almost every business in the country is a small one — 99.6% of the Philippines' 1.24 million registered establishments [8] — and unsold goods are a cost all of them quietly eat. Here's how big it gets, in three parts.

Treat these as background, not as your numbers. They come from national and global studies. They're here to show the size of the problem you're standing in. The checker never touches them — it works only on the figures you type in.

About these numbers

  • Different studies, different scopes. The food-waste totals are national and household-level, not retail-only. The inventory figures are global, not Philippine. They're shown together because they point the same direction, not because they measure the same thing.
  • Different years. Each figure carries the year of its source. The newest here is 2025; the UNEP data goes back to 2022. Nothing has been adjusted for inflation or restated.
  • One source is softer than the rest. The ~70% packaged-food share comes from a foundation and gets quoted widely in retail coverage, but it isn't official statistics. It's labelled that way rather than dropped.
  • None of this feeds the tool. The checker's answers come only from the sales figures you enter. Nothing on this page goes into the model.
