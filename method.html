Hindi ito salamangka.

No magic, no crystal ball. Here is exactly what the model was shown, what it was stopped from seeing, and what it turned out to have learned — in plain terms, so you can judge for yourself how much weight a verdict deserves.

Under the hood

How the app learned to read a shelf.

01

Fed it real history

Two full years of actual store sales — millions of purchases across thousands of products.

02

Boiled each product down

Turned every product into a few honest signals: how recently it sold, how steadily, and how many people bought it.

03

Taught it with known endings

Showed it thousands of products whose ending was already known — which kept selling, which faded — so it could learn the difference.

04

Built a wall against cheating

It only ever sees a product's first year to call its second. It cannot peek at the future, so it has to learn real patterns.

05

Tested on the unseen

Checked against products it had never met, to be sure it learned genuine signals — not lucky flukes.

What it discovered: a product's fate is written mostly in how recently and how widely it sold — not in its category or its brand. On top of that, the app learned which products get bought together (from 230,000 real baskets) and which ones actually respond to a discount — the two things that decide whether a slow item is worth saving, and how.

Before you write

Baka nasagot na dito

  • Does anything I type get uploaded?No. The checker runs entirely in your browser — the numbers you enter never leave the page. Contributions stay on this device, and messages only go out when you press send in your own email app.
  • Why did it say "don't discount"?Because the category's measured response to a price cut was not enough to cover the margin you would give up, at your price and cost. Change either and it recalculates.
  • My category isn't in the list.Pick the closest one — the health reading does not use category at all, only the bundle and discount advice does. Then tell us what is missing.
  • Can I get my data back out?Yes. Export CSV in the section above gives you everything you logged, in a file you can open in Excel.

Mga paalala · how your data and messages are handled

  • Nothing is uploaded automatically. This site is a set of static pages with no server behind them. Contributions are stored only in your own browser, and feedback is sent by opening your email app — you see the message and press send yourself.
  • Sending feedback reveals your email address to the recipient, the same as any email you send. Leave the email field blank if you do not want a reply; the message still goes through.
  • Clearing your browser data deletes your contributions. They live in this device's local storage, not in an account. Export the CSV if you want to keep them.
  • Contributed outcomes are not used automatically. Nothing you log retrains the model in your browser or anywhere else. Entries would be reviewed by hand before any future model update.
  • The training data is not Philippine. The model learned from a large foreign grocery retailer's transactions. The method transfers to a tindahan; the specific category names, pairings and discount responses may not.
  • The "match rate" is not an accuracy score. The agreement figure in your entries panel compares the app's call with what you reported on a handful of products. It is a rough personal check, not a measured evaluation of the model.
