Ang totoong bilang · The receipts

What unsold stock actually costs the Philippines.

Dead stock doesn't announce itself. It shows up as food in the bin, capital that never came back, and a shelf slot a better product could have used. Here's the scale of it — and the size of the prize for the stores that get it right. Every number is sourced and dated.

Ang tahimik na lugi

Every product that just sits there is money that's stuck.

Owners celebrate the fast movers and quietly tolerate the rest. But a slow mover isn't neutral — it charges you rent three different ways at the same time.

Frozen cash

Nakatengga ang puhunan

Money tied up in stock that won't move is money you can't spend on stock that will. The app shows the exact pesos frozen in each slow item.

Dead shelf space

Sayang ang espasyo

Every slot a loser occupies is a slot a winner can't have. In a tindahan, space is the scarcest thing you own — and slow movers hoard it.

Spoiling value

Malapit nang mag-expire

Food, sachets, and seasonal items lose value by the week. The longer they sit, the closer their worth slides to zero — then to the bin.

Isang produkto, isang desisyon

Not a dashboard. A decision.

Enter what you already know about a fading product. You get one call, in the order a store owner actually thinks: is it still fine? If not — do I push it, or do I let it go? And if I push it, how?

Question 1 · Malusog pa ba?

Is it still healthy?

If it's selling recently and steadily, you're done. Panatilihin — keep stocking it, no rescue needed. The app only escalates products that are actually fading.

Question 2 · Itulak o bitawan?

Push it, or cut it?

This is the fork everything hangs on. A slow mover is worth saving only if it has a lever you can actually pull — a discount it responds to, or a partner product it rides along with. No lever, no rescue.

Ang hindi ginagawa ng iba

It tells you when not to discount.

Every owner's reflex is to slash the price on a slow item. But plenty of products barely sell more when discounted — and on a thin sari-sari margin, a price cut can lose you money for nothing. Shelf Temp Check runs the math on your cost and price first, then stops you.

Under the hood

How the app learned to read a shelf.

It's not magic and it's not a crystal ball. Here's exactly how it was built — in plain terms.

01

Fed it real history

Two full years of actual store sales — millions of purchases across thousands of products.

02

Boiled each product down

Turned every product into a few honest signals: how recently it sold, how steadily, and how many people bought it.

03

Taught it with known endings

Showed it thousands of products whose ending was already known — which kept selling, which faded — so it could learn the difference.

04

Built a wall against cheating

It only ever sees a product's first year to call its second. It can't peek at the future, so it has to learn real patterns.

05

Tested on the unseen

Checked it against products it had never met, to be sure it learned genuine signals — not lucky flukes.

What it discovered: a product's fate is written mostly in how recently and how widely it sold — not in its category or its brand. On top of that, the app learned which products get bought together (from 230,000 real baskets) and which ones actually respond to a discount — the two things that decide whether a slow item is worth saving, and how.
