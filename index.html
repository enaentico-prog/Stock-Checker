The silent killer

Every product that just sits there is money that's stuck.

Owners celebrate their winners and quietly tolerate the losers. But dead stock isn't harmless — it costs you three ways at once.

Frozen cash

Money tied up in stock that won't move is money you can't spend on stock that will. The app shows the exact pesos frozen in each slow item.

Dead shelf space

Every slot a loser occupies is a slot a winner can't have. Space is your scarcest asset, and slow movers hoard it.

Spoiling value

Food, cosmetics, and trend items lose value by the week. The longer they sit, the closer their worth slides to zero.

One product, one verdict

Not a dashboard. A decision.

Enter what you already know about a fading product. You get one clear call — no chart to read, no numbers to stare at.

The part no one else does

It tells you when not to discount.

Every owner's reflex is to slash the price on a slow item. But nearly half of products barely sell more when discounted — and at a thin margin, a price cut can lose you money for nothing. Shelf Temp Check checks the math first and stops you.

Under the hood

How the app learned to read a shelf.

It's not magic and it's not a crystal ball. Here's exactly how it was built — in plain terms.

01

Fed it real history

Two full years of actual store sales — millions of purchases across thousands of products.

02

Boiled each product down

Turned every product into a few honest signals: how recently it sold, how steadily, and how many people bought it.

03

Taught it with endings it knew

Showed it thousands of products where the ending was already known — which kept selling, which faded — so it could learn the difference.

04

Built a wall against cheating

It only ever sees a product's first year to call its second. It can't peek at the future, so it has to learn real patterns.

05

Tested on the unseen

Checked it against products it had never met, to be sure it learned genuine signals — not lucky flukes.

What it discovered: a product's fate is written mostly in how recently and how widely it sold — not in its category or its brand. On top of that, the app learned which products get bought together (from 230,000 real baskets) and which ones actually respond to a discount — the two things that decide whether a slow item is worth saving, and how.
