Ang tahimik na lugi · the quiet loss

You already know which one it is.

The can at the back. The sachet strip nobody touches. It sold twice in March and it has been sitting there since. You keep it because throwing it out feels like admitting the money is gone — but it already is, and it is charging you rent three ways at the same time.

Frozen cash

Nakatengga ang puhunan

Money tied up in stock that will not move is money you cannot spend on stock that will. The checker shows the exact pesos frozen in each slow item.

Dead shelf space

Sayang ang espasyo

Every slot a loser occupies is a slot a winner cannot have. In a tindahan, space is the scarcest thing you own — and slow movers hoard it.

Spoiling value

Malapit nang mag-expire

Food, sachets and seasonal items lose value by the week. The longer they sit, the closer their worth slides to zero — then to the bin.

Hindi ka nag-iisa · you're not the only one

It isn't just your shelf. It's the whole ecosystem.

Slow stock feels like a personal failure when you are the one staring at it. It is not. Nearly every business in this country is a micro-enterprise — 99.6% of the Philippines' 1.24 million establishments [8] — and unsold goods are the tax they all quietly pay. Here is the size of it, in three parts.

Read this as context, not as your numbers. These figures come from national and global studies. They are here to show the shape of the problem you are standing in — the checker itself never uses them. It works only on the numbers you type in.

Mga paalala · notes on these figures

  • Different studies, different scopes. The food-waste totals are national and household-level, not retail-only. The inventory-distortion figures are global, not Philippine. They are shown together because they point the same way — not because they measure the same thing.
  • Different years. Each figure carries the year of its source. The most recent is 2025; the UNEP data references 2022. Nothing has been adjusted for inflation or restated.
  • One source is weaker than the rest. The ~70% packaged-food share is a foundation estimate that circulates widely in retail coverage. It is not official statistics, and it is labelled as such rather than dropped.
  • None of these numbers touch the tool. The checker's verdicts come only from the sales figures you enter. Nothing on this page feeds the model.
